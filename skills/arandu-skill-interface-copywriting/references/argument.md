# The argument before the layout

List the page as claims with their proof, one line per section. The claims decide which sections exist and in what order; a section with no claim is deleted. This is how a page avoids the stock sequence (hero, logo wall, three features, testimonial, pricing, CTA) that fits any product.

```markdown
# <page> — argument

Visitor: <who arrives, from where, knowing what>
Action: <the one thing the page wants>
Main objection: <what stops them>

| # | Claim (what the visitor must believe) | Proof (what exists) | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emitir uma cobrança leva menos de um minuto | demo of the real form; 3 fields | hero with the real form |
| 2 | Lembretes cobram por você | the régua de cobrança screen; e-mail and WhatsApp templates | "Régua de cobrança" |
| 3 | Você sabe o que foi pago sem abrir o banco | conciliação screen with a real-looking extract | "Conciliação" |
| 4 | O preço cabe no volume da empresa | the two plans, per cobrança/mês | pricing |
| — | "Usado por milhares de empresas" | **no evidence** | declined — unlock: real customer count with date |
```

## Claims without evidence

Do not write them and do not drop them silently. Name the claim, say why it fails, offer the closest supportable version, and state exactly what evidence would unlock the original: a customer export, a signed case, a dated metric, a certification. A declined claim with an unlock is an action item for the owner.

## The idea sentence

Before drafting, fill: "On this page, *[something only this product has]* becomes *[the device that carries the argument]*." Example: "the régua de cobrança becomes a timeline of the next 10 days of one real invoice". Apply the competitor test: if a competitor's name fits, rewrite it.
